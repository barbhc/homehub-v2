The problem

Home maintenance shouldn't feel like a second job

Most of us look after the place we live with sticky notes, a junk drawer of manuals, and reminders we keep snoozing.

What you know about your home lives in 12 places

The fridge manual is in a kitchen drawer. The warranty card is in a folder. The water heater's install date is in a text you sent yourself three years ago. Nothing is connected.

Important upkeep slips through the cracks

You changed the furnace filter last year. Or was it the year before? Without a system, the tasks you can't see are the ones that cause the damage.

Skipped upkeep turns into expensive repairs

A filter that costs a few dollars protects a furnace that costs thousands. Small jobs done on time are the cheapest repairs you'll ever make.

The solution

One system for the whole home

Homehub is built around four things that work together. You can stop after the first one and it's still useful.

Starts with your zip

Useful in your first minute

Enter a zip and Homehub tells you what your area means for upkeep: how cold your winters get and which risks run high where you live. Say what kind of home it is, and setup is done.

  • Your climate, not generic advice. A house in Minneapolis needs different care than one in Miami.
  • Just the zip, never your street.
  • Easy to correct. If Homehub gets something wrong about your area, tap Not true here.
  • Any kind of home. House, condo, townhouse or apartment.
This month's list

A few things worth doing, and nothing else

Homehub suggests a handful of tasks that fit your home this month. Where it isn't sure what you have, it asks: do you have a furnace? Is there a water heater in your unit? Nothing is added until you tap Add.

  • No model numbers needed. Standard upkeep for the things most homes have.
  • How often and how long. Every task says how often it comes around and how many minutes it takes.
  • Skip what doesn't apply. You choose what goes on your list.
  • List first, account second. You see your list before Homehub asks you to sign up.
Smart prioritization

Not all maintenance is created equal

A smoke alarm with a dead battery and a dusty refrigerator coil can both be on your list, but they aren't the same. Every task is sorted so you know what to do first and what can wait for the weekend.

Essential

Do this first

Safety and damage prevention. These are the only tasks that remind you by default.

  • Test smoke and CO alarms
Recommended

Don't put this off

Upkeep that keeps things running longer and heads off expensive repairs.

  • Flush the water heater
  • Vacuum the refrigerator coils
Optional

Good when you have time

Nice to do, with little downside if it slips a season.

  • Clean the range-hood filter
  • Wipe the refrigerator shelves
Home and Tasks

See what's next, then mark it done

Home shows the one thing to do next, what's coming up, and anything waiting on you. Mark it done or snooze it in one tap.

  • Calm reminders. Your phone only hears about the tasks you agreed to be reminded of.
  • Sort it your way. See tasks by urgency, by room, by item, or on a calendar.
  • Cleaning has its own list, so it never crowds out upkeep.
  • In a pinch. Keep the water shutoff, gas shutoff and breaker panel written down before you need them.
Items and manuals

Add a manual. Get the schedule made for it.

When you want more than the standard list, add an appliance by brand and model, or scan its label. Homehub suggests typical care right away. Add the manual and it reads it, then shows you everything it found so you can choose what to keep.

  • Typical care from day one, clearly marked as typical until the manual replaces it.
  • Upload the manual or paste a link to the PDF. Or skip it and add it later.
  • You review what it found: maintenance, cleaning, usage tips and setup steps. Nothing is saved until you press Save.
  • Warranties in one list, with what's covered and when it ends.
Ask

Your appliances have answers. Now you can ask them.

Ask the way you'd ask a friend. When Homehub has your manual, the answer comes from it and names the page. When it doesn't, you get general guidance.

  • Answers from your own manuals, with the section and page they came from.
  • Narrow it down. Point a question at one room or one item.
  • Troubleshoot when something isn't working.
  • Save the answers you'll want again.

Questions

This is version 1, so some things will be rough. If anything is confusing, broken or missing, tell me. That's the whole point of the beta.

How do I install it?

  1. On your iPhone, open the invite link.
  2. If you don't have TestFlight yet, tap View in App Store and install it, then open the invite link again.
  3. Tap Accept, then Install.
  4. Open Homehub and enter your zip.
Join the Beta

What is TestFlight?

Apple's free app for trying apps before they're in the App Store. Homehub installs and updates through it, like any other app.

Why does TestFlight say "Chief Home Officer"?

That's Homehub's old working name, still on Apple's invite page. It's the same app, and it shows up as Homehub on your phone.

How do I send feedback?

Take a screenshot while you're in Homehub, then tap Share Beta Feedback. You can also open TestFlight, pick Homehub and tap Send Beta Feedback.

Or just email chiefhomeofficerapp@gmail.com.

What does it cost?

Nothing. The beta is free, with no ads.

What do I need?

An iPhone on iOS 15 or later. There's no Android version yet.

How many people can join?

The link has room for 50 testers. If it says the beta is full, email me and I'll make room.

Who sees my home's details?

Only you and anyone you invite to your home. Read the privacy policy. To delete your account, email me and it's gone.

How do updates work?

New versions arrive in TestFlight. Each version works for 90 days, so if Homehub stops opening, check TestFlight for an update.
